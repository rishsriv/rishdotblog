# Understanding SAFEs

*Videos · Startups* · [Source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Dk6JNTDec9I)

## What is a SAFE?

- Simple Agreement for Future Equity
- Convertible security
- Receive money now, issue stock later
- Minimal negotiations
  - You decide how much money an investor will put it, and at what negotiation cap
  - Actual valuation will be decided in a priced round
- Not debt

### Main SAFE structure

THIS CERTIFIES THAT in exchange for the payment by \[Investor Name] (the **"Investor"**) of $\[\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_] (the **"Purchase Amount"**) on or about \[Date of Safe], \[Company Name], a \[State of Incorporation] corporation (the **"Company"**), issues to the investor the right to certain shares of the Company's capital stock, subject to the terms described below

This Safe is one of the forms available at [https://ycombinator.com/documents](https://ycombinator.com/documents), and the Company and Investor agree that neither one has modified the form, except to fill in blanks and bracketed terms

The "**Valuation Cap**" is $\[\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_]. See Section 2 for certain additional defined terms.

### Anatomy of the SAFE

5 pages in length. Split into 5 sections

- Section 1 - Events
  - Equity financing (company raising money)
  - Liquidity event (company selling)
  - Dissolution (company closed down)
  - Liquidation priority (who comes first in the queue to be repaid)
  - Termination
- Section 2 – Definitions
- Section 3 – company representations
- Section 4 – investor representations
- Section 5 – legal boilerplate language

### Post-Money safes

Pre-money valuation + money raised = post-money valuation

To understand how much of the company is sold:

- Founder Ownership = Amount raised / Post-money valuation cap

### Different flavors of the SAFE

- Capped (valuation is capped)
- Discount (you get a %discount on the valuation)
- Uncapped (extremely uncommon)
- Uncapped with a "most favored nation" clause (can happen if you're raising money early on — but very uncommon too)

### Dilution

Assume that – at incorporation – you just have two founders who have split up the equity equally

Next, they raise some money as Post-money safes from two different investors in two different rounds

Investor A:

- $200k, $4M post-money valuation cap
- Ownership = $200k/$4M = 5%

Investor B:

- $800k, $8M post-money valuation cap
- Ownership = $800k/$8M = 10%

Dilution for founders = 5% + 10% = 15%

When you raise some cash, you'll probably hire people. When this happens, you'll also likely issue stock + set aside an option pool. Founders will get diluted when this happens. Let's assume that the total number of shares (common shares) are 10,000,000 at this stage

A year after this, you'll have a priced round. Let's say you raise $5M ($4M from the lead investor) at a $15M pre-money valuation for your Series A. So your post-money valuation is $20M, and the target available option pool is 10% of the post-money valuation

In the priced round, 3 things happen:

1. Safes convert as shares – note that safes are included in the pre-money valuation!
2. Optional pool is increased
3. New investors invest

#### Step 1.

When the safes convert, new shares are first issued to the SAFE investors

Since they have 15% of the company (pre-money), they get an additional 1,764,705 shares (the founders 10M shares stay – dilution comes from the issuing of new shares)

- this is transitory. cap-table won't look this this. it's just a step in the calculation

If you raise at a post-money valuation that is lower than the SAFE cap, early investors will have a higher ownership than the 15% at this stage. This is why you shouldn't negotiate a crazy high valuation when getting SAFEs

#### Step 2.

Option pool increases

In this case, 1.695M additional shares are issued since the target available optional pool is 10% post money and the post-money valuation is $20M

#### Step 3.

New money invests

- Price per share = Pre-Money Valuation / Capitalization
- Capitalization = Total fully diluted shares after safe conversion and option pool increase
- Number of shares that Series A investors get = investment amount / price per share

In this case, new money capitalization = 10M original shares + 1.76M safe investor shares + 1.695M option pool increase shares = 13.455M

New money price = $15M / 13.455M shares = $1.114

Total new money shares = $5M  / $1.114 = 4.48M shares

This is the post money cap table

## Other related topics

### Safes vs convertible debt

- Safes aren't debt
- Convertible debt is more complex – maturity, interest rates etc
- Try not to have a combination of the two

### Post-money safes are recommendeed

- Pre-money safes are an option, but it's much harder to calculate dilution (specially wrt the increase in option pool)

### Don't over-optimize on market caps

- really doesn't matter all that much in terms of dilution
- In this example, if founders had negotiated for $800k on a $10M post-money safe instead of $8M, they would've owned 52.77% instead of 51.54%
