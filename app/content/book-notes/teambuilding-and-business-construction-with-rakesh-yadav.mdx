### Context

**Why is it important**: Rakesh talked about how to build a team as a technical founder, how to realign your business vision as it goes through various phases, and picking the kind of business that you want to run

### Keywords

Team Building, Fundraising, Business Evolution

### Rakesh’s background

Rakesh worked as a Senior Staff Engineer at Google as part of a 14 year stint at the company. He started a company called [JarvisML](https://jarvisml.com) in March 2021, which helps enterprises increase their revenue via machine learning driven nudges aimed at users

### Teambuilding as a technical founder

- Rakesh is still very closely involved with engineering
- You should eventually scale up and delegate. But at the start, just do it yourself
- You will attract different kind of talent once you have that kind of programming cred. Being a good programmer that really knows what they're talking about and why they're doing things in a certain way leads to talent that really wants to work with you
- Have a personal and organizational pursuit for excellence in tech and team-building. Best way to attract crazy amazing talent
- Rakesh has helped people build up their career for a long time, so he has a lot of credibility with his team. They know that he has their best interest in mind. That's where young founders (like me) have a uphill battle
- Currently, Rakesh spends one week a quarter coding. But is involved with everything and has a lot of visibility. He prefers to code out infrastructure himself because that has the best leverage

### **Getting the right partners and mentors**

- For technical people, the struggle will often come from hiring non-tech people
- Rakesh's personal hack => just leverage the right partners and mentors on board. **But don't get random advisors who don't give you time**

### **On constantly realigning the business/vision, and pitching to investors**

- Spend a lot of time thinking about what the real problem is and whether it's worth solving. Do this due diligence every 3 months
- Crazy important to pick a problem that's big enough. Don't not take a salary. Don't sacrifice your personal life. Biggest challenge will be scaling the business
- In terms of "tactical advice", the only people that really matter are the customers that are paying you. Don't listen to anything that goes against what you're getting from your customers
- If the market is right, you can negotiate a lot more. So you don't necessarily have to wait for revenue before raising
- Revenue matters a lot less in the early phases. Strategy/market/team matters a heck of a lot more

### **On picking the kind of business you want to run**

- Two types of businesses: lifestyle and venture
- Revenue matters a lot more for lifestyle businesses
- Startups are built on promises (specially at the early stage). Market size and strategy has to be there
- If you don't have cash in the bank but find a promising market and start generating revenue, someone who is better funded can just copy and kill you (and undercut you on price). So secure the bag first.
- Either do lifestyle or do startup. Don't do anything intermediate

### **General operational advice to me as a technical founder**

- Don't ask people to work part-time for. You'll burn your relationships if you do that if this causes burnout in them
- Secure the money first, then hire the people you want to hire
- Drop all the technical work you're doing right now, and focus a lot more on fundraising instead. You are good at tech and can build the product whenever you want. Instead, focus on hiring a great team (for which you need funds). Teambuilding is not an "I owe you" kind of teambuilding – build a team with the right structure. Give them a decent salary. Just raise some damn funds.
- REALLY need to find someone on the business side. Very difficult to do both business and tech together
