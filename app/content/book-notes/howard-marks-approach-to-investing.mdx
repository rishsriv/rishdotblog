# Howard Marks' Approach to Investing

*Podcasts · Investing* · [Source](http://whatgotyouthere.com/234-howard-marks-episode-transcript/)

## Summary

### Approach to investing

Don't search for something intelligent or different to do every day. Just be aware of excesses in the environment and the extremes of the economic cycle, market, or investor behavior. When that happens, stand away from the herd (buy when the herd is selling, sell when the herd is buying)

There are **few things so bad that they can’t be under-priced and a good idea**. It’s not what you buy, it’s what you pay. And the corollary of that is that good investing is not a matter of buying good things, it’s a matter of buying things well

Pattern is to find when things are overpriced, which is usually a function of there being too much optimism and excesses in the physical environment, or when they are underpriced (too much pessimism when there are craters in the environment)

There are 3 stages to a bull market:

- **first stage** when very few people, only a gifted few understand that there could be improvement\*\*.\*\* that’s when you get bargains
- **second stage**, when most people accept that improvement is actually taking place
- **third stage**, when everybody believes that things can only get better forever. That's when you can really get killed if you participate

### Stoicism

Emotional control, un-emotionality is essential, because if you are subject to the same emotions as the herd, then you’ll probably do the same things as the herd, not stand away

### Pattern recognition

When he was young, he hadn’t seen so many cycles and didn’t have the pattern recognition ability. May not have had the confidence to make important decisions

Pattern recognition lets you get to the point where you say, `oh, it’s one of those`.

### This time is different?

When the market goes to extremes, people always say, oh, this time it’s different, in other words, the old rules don’t apply, this is such a unique experience that the old rules don’t apply, and so the old valuation limitations. **When you hear people say this time is different, a red light should go on**.

I think the phrase came from Sir John Templeton, "sometimes it is different, 20% of the time, it really is different, and the world does change", and if that was true when he said it in the 80s, **I think it’s much more true today, maybe it’s 40% or 50%**

So it’s much more likely today that things really are different, that was one of the themes of my latest memo, something of value, and so I think it’s important to recognize that today and be more flexible in our approach

### Valuation

There are quantitative things as well, valuations, PE ratios, bond yields, yield spreads, capitalization rates on real estate, enterprise values on private equity purchase, these are quantitative, but they can mislead, and especially now in this period of great change. The historic valuation ratios may not be that meaningful

### Being right early = being wrong

Being right early looks like being wrong, and the question is, can you survive in that interim between when you make the decision and when it’s proved out?

These can be very difficult times and you can show terrible performance and lose most of your money under management, even with a decision, which is right, in principle

### Make fewer decisions

You can’t make a good decision every day. These calls of a peak or a trough at the extreme of bubble and crash or high and low bull and bear – the decisions, the logic is clear and compelling, and the probabilities are on your side, the probability of being right is high. Make these important decisions a few times a decade

If you do it in between, you try to do it every day or every week or every month. You try to do it when the market is 2% over value to 4% under value. It’s very hard to be right

### Building a team of collaborators

It helps not to do these things on your own, but to do them in partnership with somebody else who sees the world the way you do. Lind of like Buffet and Munger or something like that

Oaktree Capital's founders were not newcomers to each other or to the business, and had been very successful already. Had made enough money so they could live with the risk if it didn’t work.

Had been successful together, and had kind of proved out our methodology and developed reputations which are important in the investment business, so deciding to go and do it on their own was not enormously risky

#### Have the same values

If you work with people who have different values than you do, it’s unlikely to be successful. It’s gonna be riven by the battle between the cowboys and the chickens. When you go through a bull market, the cowboys say the chickens are holding us back. And when it turns into a bear market, the chickens say the Cowboys are getting us killed.

#### Have complementary skills

If your skills are duplicative and you have the same strengths, then you probably have the same weaknesses. What ends up happening is one person eventually says, "Well, I can do everything he does and more, I don’t need him, and so he says, I want you to go cut your share, I need more", and that jeopardizes the organization

### How long do you hold on to a winner?

The hardest thing in the world is to hold a stock for 25 years and have it see it go from one to 10 to a 100 to a thousand and not sell it, and then watch it go to 10000 and 100000

That’s how you make the big money, but you have to hold for a long time. Instead, most people sell

I’ve always had this rule that if you sell half – you can’t be all wrong, but you also can’t be all right. The cautious seldom error or write great poetry

### Winning in bear markets

The things that work the best are the things that are unconventional and correct

When you avoid the pain of the collapse, then you have your wits about you and can swing into action to take advantage of the bargains that are created by the carnage. If you are being hurt every day, and your portfolio is breaking down and your companies are turning into basket cases requiring remediation, you can’t become aggressive – you’re both too busy, and you’re too scarred to be able to turn aggressive

### On Writing Memos

Has been doing it for 30 years. In the first 10 years, never had a response. Kept going because:

1. It’s his creative outlet, he loves to write
2. Has learnt so much from writing. A lot of the things that he thinks are there because he thought of them during the writing process (writing helps formulate better thoughts)

There are two kinds of memos:

1. In the moment an event happens, where it’s my job to explain this event to his clients
2. Timeless memos which are important in their substance – which are kind of like projects. For these, he accumulates information clippings, piles them up, and starts writing when he is ready to go. These can take one to three months because he feels no hurry and wants do a thorough job
