## Summary

### A. Core Philosophy

> 💡 Use massive initial investment (fixed costs), efficient systems, and usage at scale to bring down marginal costs of serving new customers close to zero, and pass down savings to customers. Use this to attract even more customers to the platform – and eventually become profitable with deep moats

Have ensured that as their network grew,  their system would get more efficient, and then they will get better and better for all of our customers, being able to deliver more and more return on their investment with us. As more people use Cloudflare's network, the network itself improves, and the data feeds back on itself.

#### Example of this in action

Started out protecting the content side of the network from security threats, DDoS attacks etc. But increasingly, also protecting the user side of the network with products like Cloudflare for Teams

### B. Differentiating Factors

1. Product and engineering structure (Emerging Technologies Group separate from the rest of the team)
2. Broad customer base with free customers that lets them innovate faster than competitors
3. Nimble development platform – Cloudflare Workers

#### B1. Product and Engineering Structure

About 10% of our product and engineering org is dedicated to new products. This is ETI, or emerging technology and incubation. They are not measured on immediate product adoption,  revenue, or what's going to be happening in the next quarter. Instead, they're focused on what the long-term opportunity is.

Cloudflare Workers and Teams both came out of ETI

#### B2. Broad Customer Base

Use broad customer base to develop products more quickly. Free customers allow them to develop and test products + make them reliable  faster.

Reach out to our free customers whenever they want to test a new feature in production. Because of this, were the first ones to have be deploying HTTP/2 at scale, the first ones to be deploying HTTP/3 at scale, the first ones to push the new cryptography standards like TLS 1.3, and then take these to enterprise quickly.

#### B3. Cloudflare Workers

Built internally to make development at Cloudflare faster. Used something they called the Bezos rule to build this.

> 💡 **Bezos Rule**: Any API or any development tool that we build for ourselves and for our own team, we also are then going to make available to our customers.

Since 2017, have been working on what is the most stable, fastest, most performant, most scalable edge computing platform in the world.

#### B4. All together: an integrated network as the computer

With these, you get a feedback machine – which is constantly looking for new markets and new ways to satisfy customers and delivers more value to them. It's like an exponential TAM development across our network. This algorithm keeps feeding back on itself, to open doors to new markets and disrupt new industries.

At Cloudflare's core is our network. And our network is really special. Everything else flows from the network.

### C. The Flywheels

#### C1. Network Flywheel

The network flywheel means that as there is a bigger audience, they provide more services, and are then invited to be in more and more networks around the world. They are also able to drive down their costs, and that helps them deliver more ROI to our customers and be able to deliver better and better services over time.

#### C2. Product Flywheel

The interlinked product flywheel does the same thing, but with products, Because they're serving a global audience, they're able to better develop a broad range of products that use our global network and then are able to be delivered across that network at an incredibly effective rate because of how efficient the underlying network is.

### D. Go-to-market

#### D1. Developer First

Build products, first and foremost, for developers. Have APIs to control all products. In the dashboard, right on every single page, there's a link to the APIs that control every single function across Cloudflare.

Developers want to be able to sign up and kick the tires without having to talk to a salesperson necessarily and only call a salesperson when they're really serious about having seen something. They need great documentation. And so all of these things are things that Cloudflare prioritizes.

The vast majority of their users have been developers. And those developers are becoming more and more influential in IT organizations. Because they already have a relationship with those developers (because developers have tried out their products), when they launch new products like Cloudflare Workers, they're able to address them, get in front of them and being their trust in a way that is harder for other platforms.

#### D2. Data-driven, inverted GTM

Instead of organizations that go to market by starting with marketing, then adding sales and then eventually getting customers — they get customers early on, get those lighthouse customers, get those reference customers that often are early adopters, sign up because they're developer-led organizations and become real advocates

### E. Products

#### E0. Fundamental Layer: The Network

These are a CDN server that is in 200+ cities, including relatively far-flung places like Pakistan and Kenya. These act as the building block for everything else.

#### E1. Cybersecurity Products

Using data collected from many years to block DDOS attacks, provide a firewall against malicious attacks, protect data in transit, protect individuals in companies from intrusions etc.

#### E2. Web Performance Products

Faster web loading, faster routing, rate limiting, video delivery, load balancing etc

#### E3. Developer Products (not included in TAM calculations)

CloudFlare Workers and it's associated suite of products. An edge computing competitor to AWS, GCP, Azure etc. This includes:

- Cloudflare Workers (essentially Lambda + API Gateway)
- KV Store (essentially DynamoDB @ edge)
- Durable Objects (for maintaining states, and useful where you need immediate consistency instead of eventual consistency)
- *Coming soon – not publicly announced yet*: SQL\@edge
- *Coming soon – not publicly announced yet*: Kafka\@edge
